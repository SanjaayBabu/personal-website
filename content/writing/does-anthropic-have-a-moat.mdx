As many of you may know, Anthropic PBC is poised to start trading publicly in one of the biggest IPOs of all time. This will be yet another astronomical IPO, following SpaceX's public offering in June 2026. As people are deciding whether to buy into the ANT IPO, the biggest question that has popped up is whether Anthropic has a sufficient moat.

# What's a moat?

Basic economics would tell you that if a company is currently enjoying supernormal profits, then that creates an incentive for other companies to enter the market. Businesses need to have something special that would make it hard for another business to come in and steal their lunch. There are several ways to build a moat:

- **Regulatory monopoly power** — cronyism or corruption could make it impossible for new entrants to capture market share.
- **Network effects** — if you and all your friends use Goodreads, it would be hard for a new entrant to come in and take market share from Goodreads. Or consider the switching costs of moving away from an Apple product because of the ecosystem they built.
- **Brand loyalty** — usually applies to luxury goods.

# The bar is high

As Noahpinion wrote in his piece about Anthropic's moat (business parlance for a barrier to entry that might allow for positive long-run profits), the high initial selling price makes Anthropic a difficult buy. For Anthropic to be as profitable an investment today as Google was when it went public, it would have to be worth $200 to $260 trillion by the late 2040s. This is HUGE, and I don't think that needs much justifying.

Again, as Noahpinion wrote in [his excellent piece](https://www.noahpinion.blog/p/anthropics-moat-is-claude), for this to happen, two things need to happen: (a) the economic pie more broadly needs to grow, and (b) Anthropic needs to be able to capture a bigger share of it. Many in the AI optimist camp would argue that, indeed, the economic pie is going to grow significantly. So even if we believe that the first condition will be met, the remaining question is whether new entrants can take Anthropic's market share.

# The bear case

The bearish case is that Anthropic doesn't have a moat. Open source models are often just weeks behind Anthropic's flagship models, and are far cheaper to run. Also, ANT doesn't produce its own compute, which is a huge risk as competitors could monopolize compute. And far from enjoying a government-sponsored monopoly, ANT could face regulatory obstacles that could threaten future profits.

I was fairly convinced by SPCX because I think they are the most likely to capitalize on the network effects of owning compute (the Colossus cluster), energy (potentially through SpaceX and space data centers), and enormous amounts of training data from X and Tesla. Of course, the multiple for a SPCX investment probably won't be that crazy, but they also have the added moat of monopoly power from leading a very large number of space launches.

# Why I'm semi-convinced about ANT

I think there are pretty important network effects within AI as well, even if they don't look the same as social media. You aren't likely to use an Anthropic model just because your friends are. The network effects, in my view, are more similar to Apple's, where there are so many added benefits to picking Anthropic and staying with them. As Anthropic builds its enterprise user base and collects more and more data from non-ZDR users that feeds into training runs, they are likely to create skills and packages that benefit businesses — consider the Claude Finance and Legal packages, which have been hugely useful.

**Most importantly, I think laziness / default bias is a moat.** We often forget that much of our economy is built on smaller businesses that want a product that works out of the box — they don't really want to tinker with open models, which I think appeal more to hobbyists who want to create their own intelligence. Laziness leads some businesses to somewhat irrational decisions — e.g. paying inordinate amounts of money for Salesforce / Oracle SaaS offerings when they probably could hire an intern who could build something decently fit for their needs. So even if the cost of building an open source system is somewhat lower, they're not likely to do it.

The laziness moat might also protect Anthropic from immediate competition from OpenAI. At times in 2026, OpenAI's models have been somewhat better on some benchmarks, but there isn't much evidence that Anthropic's enterprise market share has changed much. I think this is just businesses not wanting to constantly change their providers.

My final argument is a little shakier. If open source models were really going to steal Anthropic's lunch, they already should have. I don't think most businesses need the kind of frontier intelligence that Fable or Astra models have; most open models would be able to do the same work. But Anthropic has been very successful at monetizing nonetheless.

# The biggest risks

The biggest risk to ANT's business, I think, is constraints on chips, energy, data and talent. I don't know enough to weigh in on these, and will leave other market participants to weigh in on them. But broadly, I'm confident that if the US is going to let its model of private competition do its thing to ensure that the US stays ahead in the so-called AI race, then it will do what it needs to prevent anti-competitive restrictions on chips and energy. There is an industry-wide downside risk that energy bottlenecks prevent the kind of scaling that ANT needs for its enormous market share in 2040, which I won't understate.

All in all, I think Anthropic does indeed have a somewhat robust moat, though with some crucial vulnerabilities.
