I've had a productive but restful summer, and now I'm back in full academic swing! I wanted to take some time out to reflect on what I've decided to study this semester, and share my reasoning and expectations from the various classes I'm scheduled to take.

# Math 212: Multivariable Calculus

Multivariable calculus is the foundation behind most economic / econometric models. I decided to take this class first before doing linear algebra for a very simple reason — the latter takes more time, and I don't have too much of that this semester! 

I do look forward to a fun time in multivariable calculus. I just came out of my first class, and it seems like a different way of doing calculus, one defined by vectors rather than scalar numbers. I hope that this class prepares me well for my advanced Econometrics class (ECON 204), and hopefully gives me a strong foundation in the math that economic models make use of. 

# Econ 201: Microeconomics

I'm fully convinced that Economics will be my primary major. I love the tools of analysis it provides, and I love the blend of theory and quantitative rigor. Econ 201 is the first of three more advanced economics courses, which starts to introduce the math that defines modern economics. 

Through this course,  I hope to stop thinking subjectively (and sometimes normatively) about the economy, and start thinking about the various parameters in economic problems. I also have a good feeling about this course. Looking through the syllabus, it seems to have a wide scope, covering basic consumer/producer theory, strategic interactions, social consequences of consumer/producer behaviour, and game theory. 

# Bass Connections: Chinese Law & Policy

China is a growing power, and is a geographic reality in Singapore. However, I don't understand Chinese law & legal institutions very much at all — this contrasts with my (decent) understanding of lawmaking in the US, UK and the EU. Knowledge on Chinese law, particularly research done in English, is also very fragmented. It is this problem that my Bass Connections project seeks to address. 

Bass Connections is a Duke program in which you work with researchers and graduate students to tackle a research problem. My project is with Duke Law School, a good fit given that legal scholarship is the core focus here. I'm excited to work with a strong team on an interesting, pertinent problem!

# Financial Economics 390: Macro Analytics

How is the economy doing? This is a question that most Economics majors feel unprepared to answer. We spend so much time in theory, that we sometimes forget to connect this with the real world around us. 

This course on Macro Analytics goes against this grain. In this course, I'll learn how to pull & analyze macroeconomic data to make assessments and potentially forecasts about where the economy is going. 

I'm really excited for this class because I think it will ground my theoretical understanding of economics with an empirical toolkit. We'll be learning how to integrate theory with data through building & refining models on Excel. 

I also picked this class as a nice complement to the work I'll be doing this semester as a presenter on Duke's college Federal Reserve Challenge team. This competition involves us doing research into, and presenting a video on, the current state of the U.S. economy and making a recommendation on what to do with interest rates. 

# Russian 101

Taking three semesters of language is required at Duke (and most US colleges). I actually really like this requirement. 

As someone who hopes to work in the world of international relations and national security, the language I choose to do is an important decision. Languages open up a whole new world; and a part of the world I know very little about and hope to learn more about is Russia & Eastern Europe. They obviously have a deep, rich history filled with twists and turns. Modern Russia is a product of this history, and there is so much interesting literature & art that it has produced. 

I wanted to learn Russian because of this geographical interest, as well as my general desire to learn a language with a non-Latin script, just for the challenge. I've done my first lesson, and I'm really liking it so far!
